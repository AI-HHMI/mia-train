# Does labelled-data scaling on NISB pay off at a larger window?

`data_scaling` found NISB `base` flat from 1 to 5 training cubes at a 256³ window (whole-cube nERL
0.423 / 0.431 / 0.391). One explanation is the generator: one cube already spans what five span.
Another is the window. A 256³ crop of a synthetic volume is easy to exhaust (one cube holds ~700
disjoint ones), so more cubes might help a model that sees more context at once. This experiment
runs the same 1 → 3 → 5 series at 256³ and at 512³ (~90 disjoint crops per cube) with one model. If
512³ gives a signal, a larger window comes next.

| arm | window (x, y, z voxels) | physical extent | training cubes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `w256_1cube` | 256³ | 2.3 × 2.3 × 5.1 µm | `seed0` |
| `w256_3cube` | 256³ | | `seed0-2` |
| `w256_5cube` | 256³ | | `seed0-4` |
| `w512_1cube` | 512³ | 4.6 × 4.6 × 10.2 µm | `seed0` |
| `w512_3cube` | 512³ | | `seed0-2` |
| `w512_5cube` | 512³ | | `seed0-4` |

The cube sets are `data_scaling`'s: nested, in seed order, with equal weights. Every arm validates on
`seed100` at its own window. NISB's voxels are 9 × 9 × 20 nm, and each cube is 3000 × 3000 × 1350
voxels.

## One model

The model is gary_comparison arm 5a: the released DINOv3 ViT-L/16 (LVD-1689M) inflated to 3D, with
global attention in every block, trained with `affinity_seg` and the UNETR decoder, using 5a's
recipe, augmentation and 500k steps. Two changes were requested (2026-10-02):
- batch 1 per rank × 8 ranks on one B300 node, where 5a ran 4 × 2; the global batch is 8 in both;
- a checkpoint every 100k steps, where 5a saved every 50k.

The configs are generated by `make_configs.py`, so the arms differ only in the window and the cube
list. `diff <(grep -v '^#' w256_1cube.toml) <(grep -v '^#' w512_5cube.toml)` shows those lines and
nothing else. One of them is `decode_chunks`: at 512³ the UNETR head decodes in 4 slabs along x,
which keeps its full-resolution tensors under cuDNN's 2^31-element limit and computes the same
logits as an undivided decode (`UNETRHead`, `tests/unit/test_affinity_chunked.py`; bit-identical
in fp32 at 512³, `probes/unetr_slabs/` on /nrs).

## Attention score cap

Every arm sets `attn_max_score = 1e5`: queries and keys are shortened where needed so that no attention
score exceeds 1e5 (`layers.common.attention.cap_lengths`). The first launch (2026-10-02) had no cap.
Its gradient norms grew from ~0.1 to 1e5-1e12 in all six arms, and two had collapsed when all six
were killed on 2026-10-03; their checkpoints and run directories are deleted.

- **Cause:** block 0's attention went one-hot, and its largest score grew from 577 (released model)
  to 3e8. The fused attention kernels (cuDNN in bf16, the memory-efficient one in fp32) give wrong
  gradients at such scores: on the same weights and samples, PyTorch's exact reference attention
  gave normal gradients. In bf16 the gradients were already 2.5x off at 7.7e5, while the logged
  grad norm still looked normal.
- **Why 1e5:** the released model's longest query or key on NISB and hemibrain crops (334-356)
  needs 1.6e4, so the cap is inactive at the start. On gary_comparison 5a at 500k, it brings the
  bf16 gradient error from 64% to 14%. Float32 attention in blocks 0-1 was tested too: no better
  overall, still wrong at 3.7e8 without a cap, and +55% step time at 512^3.
- **Cost:** at most 1.5% step time at either window.

Measurements and scripts: `probes/grad_blowup/` on /nrs.

## Cost

Measured 2026-10-02 on one B300 node: 8 ranks, compiled, a synthetic batch (`capability_sweep.py`,
`probes/unetr_slabs/`).

| window | s/step | peak memory per GPU | 500k steps |
|---|---|---|---|
| 256³ | 0.21 | 18.5 GiB | ~29 h |
| 512³ | 1.24 | 127 GiB | ~7.2 days |

At 512³, 3 slabs ran at 1.28 s/step, and activation checkpointing would cost 10% (1.41 s at 3
slabs, 87 GiB) for memory the arms don't need. The one-GPU smoke runs also exercised the real data
pipeline. With its 2 workers the 512³ smoke barely waited for data (<0.1% of the time), while the
256³ smoke waited ~25%. The real runs have 11 workers per GPU; 1a, at the same crop and batch,
waited 0.3% with 8.

## Reading the result

Pre-registered (2026-10-02), from nERL on the test cube:
- **Supported** if nERL rises with cubes at 512³: 5 cubes beat 1 cube by more than 0.05, with
  3 cubes in between or above 1. The reference is the 256³ series re-run here; in `data_scaling`
  that difference was −0.03.
- **Not supported** if the 512³ curve is flat within 0.05, about the spread of the 256³ series.
- **Validity check:** 5 cubes at 512³ should beat 5 cubes at 256³. If they do not, the model is not
  using the extra context, and a flat slope says nothing about the generator.

There is one seed per arm. If wall-clock allows, a second seed for the 1- and 5-cube 512³ arms
would show whether a gap of 0.05 is real.

## Running it

    bash experiments/large_inputs_nisb/submit.sh w256_1cube w256_3cube w256_5cube w512_1cube w512_3cube w512_5cube
    bash experiments/large_inputs_nisb/submit.sh --dry-run <arm> ...    # print the bsub lines

One full B300 node per arm. Each real run waits on a 20-step, one-GPU smoke run. Outputs go to
`/nrs/scicompsoft/orhane/mia-train-experiments/large_inputs_nisb/` (`runs/`, `jobs/`, smoke runs
under `*/smoke/`).

## Scoring

Scoring uses mia-evals' `nisb_base_neurite_tracing` task (test `seed101`, route `mws_blockwise`),
and runs only on request. Every arm predicts with `predict.py --cover-box`, which covers the whole
cube at either window, so all six rows share one region. The 512³ arms predict at their own window
(`--patch 512`; `predict.py` refuses a window other than the model's `img_size`), and `logits()`
decodes in the run's 4 slabs.
